# Sell inspection readiness, not more software

ECCS should stop widening the app and instead become the one vendor that keeps a Hyderabad kitchen ready for a raid, with the app as the proof of work rather than the product. The research supports this from four directions. Enforcement in Hyderabad now means same-day closure and press coverage rather than fines, and inspectors are citing missing records as violations in their own right. Restaurants spend several times more on hygiene services than on software, and base restaurant software in India sells for under Rs 1,000 per outlet per month. No competitor found, in India or abroad, combines cleaning, pest control, scored inspections and a restaurant-side compliance app. And only 361 Hyderabad eateries hold an FSSAI hygiene rating, so the one official badge that exists is almost unclaimed. The practical programme is: about eight to nine working weeks of cheap app work that makes the hygiene score harder to fake and closer to what inspectors check; a handful of packaged services (FSSAI rating readiness, scheduled exhaust cleaning with a certificate, oil testing, and partner-delivered water tests, staff medicals and supervisor training); service plans with the app bundled in, sold first to chains' Hyderabad clusters, cloud kitchens, caterers and food courts; and a firm no to sensors, CCTV analytics, standalone software subscriptions and becoming the official auditor. The evidence has real holes: almost every local price in this report is a listing price or missing altogether and needs phone quotes, and nothing shows that a private hygiene score moves diners the way government grades do.

## Raids close kitchens, while software budgets stay under Rs 1,000 a month

**The penalty that matters in Hyderabad in 2026 is closure and publicity.** Telangana merged its food safety and drug control departments into TG-SAFE, and a police task force (H-FAST) runs alongside it ([The News Minute](https://www.thenewsminute.com/telangana/telangana-merges-food-safety-drug-control-depts-under-tg-safe)). On 24 September 2026, eight teams inspected 15 establishments and **suspended eight FSSAI licences with immediate closure**; the listed violations included cockroach and rodent infestation, expired products and "inadequate pest-control documentation" ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/tg-safe-orders-eight-eateries-to-shut-over-serious-hygiene-lapses)). A 10 September drive across 46 establishments suspended three licences and cited unclean refrigerators with poor segregation and labelling, and record-keeping violations ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/food-safety-teams-inspect-46-establishments-suspend-licences-of-three-1986508)). Well-known brands appear on these lists: Chutneys, Ohri's, Shah Ghouse and Pista House outlets were inspected on 29 August ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/tg-safe-raids-seven-restaurants-in-hyderabad-finds-food-safety-violations)). Money penalties, by contrast, are close to nil: Telangana recorded **zero penalties and zero convictions in 2025-26 against 398 failed samples**, with 44 of 81 food safety officer posts filled ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/398-telangana-food-samples-fail-tests-but-0-penalties-convictions-3521827/)). The inference is that enforcement arrives in drives, with police, concentrated inside the Outer Ring Road, and that day-to-day readiness matters more than preparing for a scheduled visit.

**The same short list of failures repeats in every drive**: expired or undated stock, pests, dirty refrigerators without segregation or labels, spoiled produce, poor handler hygiene, missing records, and no valid licence. ECCS's current app covers some of this (checklists, licence vault) and its planned food labels cover the most-cited item. It does not yet hold the records inspectors ask for: pest-control visit logs, staff medical certificates, water test reports, oil disposal records and the supervisor's training certificate, all of which appear in GHMC's October 2024 instructions for larger establishments ([Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/news/hyderabad-mandates-cctv-in-kitchens-for-food-safety-1828317)).

**The 2026 licence reforms weaken one of ECCS's existing features.** From 1 April 2026, FSSAI raised the basic registration ceiling from Rs 12 lakh to Rs 1.5 crore turnover and made licences perpetual, per legal explainers rather than the Gazette text ([Velco Legal](https://velcolegalindia.com/blog/fssai-2026-amendments-complete-guide); [Setindiabiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/fssai-2026-reforms)). The sources disagree on the notification date and on whether existing licences convert, and whether obligations worded for "licensees" still bind an outlet now reclassified to registration is unclear. The inference for ECCS is that FSSAI licence expiry stops being the natural reminder and sales trigger, and the 10 score points given to licences need a new basis. The dates that remain are the municipal trade licence (calendar year, with a 25% penalty rising to 50% for late renewal, per the [GHMC 2026 press note](https://www.ghmc.gov.in/quicklinks/Press%20Note%20for%20Trade%20License%20Renewal%20for%20year%202026.pdf)), annual staff medicals, annual or half-yearly water tests, pest-control visits, and the two-year hygiene rating. One secondary source reports a July 2026 municipal direction telling Swiggy and Zomato to list only outlets holding both a valid FSSAI document and a valid trade licence ([Anantam IAS](https://anantamias.com/current-affairs/food-delivery-platforms-unlicensed-outlets/)); if that holds, a lapsed trade licence is a revenue risk for delivery-led kitchens.

**The money is in services.** Petpooja's base POS lists at Rs 10,000 per outlet for the first year and Rs 7,000 to 7,500 on renewal, per a reseller listing ([Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/detail/petpooja-pos)). Against that, one vendor's deep-cleaning rate card runs Rs 4 to 7 per sq ft ([Hygiene Dunia](https://hygienedunia.com/shop/commercial-restaurant-deep-cleaning-cost-1500sf/)) and an FSSAI hygiene rating audit costs Rs 9,500 to 15,000 ([Equinox Labs](https://equinoxlab.com/hygiene-rating-audit-fssai.php)). The researchers' arithmetic puts a 1,500 sq ft restaurant's annual hygiene-services spend several times above its base software spend. Pure-software vendors in this category have mostly ended as modules of bigger companies: Jolt sold to Digi for about US$145.5 million ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250818839282/en/Digi-Acquires-Jolt-to-Accelerate-ARR-Growth-and-Strengthen-SmartSense%E2%80%99s-Market-Leading-Offerings)), and Checkit sits at break-even on about £14 million of recurring revenue ([FY26 trading update](https://eqs-news.com/news/results-and-trading-reports/fy26-trading-update/5d1112f9-e53b-4219-8f2d-dd95cd285737_en)). In India the one comparable exit was services-led: HiCare reached about Rs 80 crore of revenue and was bought by Rentokil PCI ([Rentokil Initial](https://www.rentokil-initial.com/media/news-releases/2024/india.aspx)).

**ECCS's bundle is unmatched, but each half has a serious rival.** Petpooja Tasks already offers photo-proof checklists with AI photo checking, geofencing, WhatsApp alerts, 150+ templates and seven regional languages, inside a POS that claims 100,000+ restaurants ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/how-petpooja-tasks-work/)). Its own page mentions no services, licence vault, hygiene score or third-party inspection. On the service side, Hyderabad's Dasha Food Safety sells a monthly compliance retainer with weekly inspections and claims 200+ kitchens and clients such as Pista House, with no visible software and no published price ([Dasha Food Safety](https://dashafoodsafety.com/)). Local duct cleaners such as My Kitchen Experts offer AMCs, "NFPA 96 compliance certificates" and before-and-after photos, with no app ([My Kitchen Experts](https://mykitchenexperts.in/service/commercial-kitchen-duct-cleaning-service/)). ECCS will not out-distribute Petpooja on checklists; its edge is that it has technicians and an office board watching the data. The closest outside evidence favours that combination: in a UK Food Standards Agency pilot, two of three restaurants using digital records with a provider monitoring entries rose from ratings of 1 and 2 to 4 ([FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/document/kitchenlogs-and-harrow-council-feasibility-study-report.pdf)). Three restaurants is a direction, not a number.

## Nine weeks of cheap builds make the score believable

The hygiene score is ECCS's core asset, and 30 of its 100 points rest on self-reported checklists. The first priority is to make that part trustworthy and to make the app hold what an inspector asks for. All effort figures below are the researchers' estimates for a founder plus an AI assistant, not sourced benchmarks.

| Priority | Build | Effort (working weeks) | Cash cost | Verify first |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Photo integrity: in-app camera only, server time, duplicate-photo detection, location and fake-GPS flags | 1 to 2 | Nil | Indoor GPS error at real customer sites |
| 2 | Corrective actions: a failed item becomes a task with owner, deadline, proof and escalation | 2 | Nil | Who at the restaurant owns the task |
| 3 | Compliance file: pest visit log, staff medicals, water tests, supervisor certificate, trade licence, fire NOC, all with reminders | About 1 (this report's estimate; reuses the vault) | Nil | Which records apply to registration-only outlets |
| 4 | Temperature log with limits and alerts | 1 | Nil | Schedule 4 wording on temperatures |
| 5 | Used-oil log, with readings taken by ECCS technicians | 1 | About Rs 44,000 for one meter | Cheaper test strips; RUCO status in Hyderabad |
| 6 | Checklist behaviour flags on the office board | 2 | Nil | None |
| 7 | Equipment register with QR stickers and recurring-visit scheduling | 3 to 5 | Stickers | Sticker material that survives degreaser |
| 8 | WhatsApp reminders and reports | 2 | About Rs 1,200 a month per 100 outlets plus provider fee | Meta's rate card and provider fees |
| 9 | Audio playback of SOPs | 1 | Small | Voice quality in each of the 12 languages |

**Photo integrity comes first because it is cheap and protects everything else.** Vendors in the category treat camera-only capture, timestamps, GPS proof and pattern detection as the standard toolkit against "pencil whipping", meaning ticking a checklist without doing the work ([Xenia](https://www.xenia.team/articles/pencil-whipping); [Jolt](https://jolt.com/?p=66971)). Libraries exist to detect faked location in React Native ([react-native-turbo-mock-location-detector](https://www.jsdelivr.com/package/npm/react-native-turbo-mock-location-detector)). Location mismatches should flag for review, not block, because GPS in basements and malls is unreliable. No source quantifies how much checklist fraud occurs or how much these controls cut it; this is standard practice, not proven effect. Behaviour flags (a long checklist finished in two minutes, every day "all pass") belong on the office board as a reliability indicator, not as an accusation shown to the customer.

**Corrective actions and the compliance file turn the app into inspection readiness.** Closed-loop corrective actions are standard across global products ([Operandio](https://operandio.com/jolt-alternatives/)) and absent from the ECCS brief. They also produce sales signals: repeated grease-trap failures suggest a booking. The compliance file is the cheapest answer to "inadequate pest-control documentation", and in India it is otherwise sold as human labour by firms like Dasha. The FSSAI hygiene rating guidance sets one trained supervisor per 25 food handlers and annual water and food testing for licensees ([FSSAI guidance](https://hygiene.fssai.gov.in/resources/Guidance_Document_Hygiene_Rating.pdf)). Do not set a reminder for supervisor-certificate expiry yet: FSSAI's own June 2023 clarification says the certificate is perpetual ([FSSAI clarification](https://fostac.fssai.gov.in/fostacV2/downloadnotification/Clarification%20regarding%20FSS%20Validity.pdf)), while training providers claim two or three years.

**Temperature and oil logs are regulatory needs that reuse the checklist engine.** Secondary sources give fridges at 1 to 5°C, hot holding above 60°C and twice-daily fridge logs ([Velco Legal](https://velcolegalindia.com/blog/temperature-control-food-safety-india)); the regulation text was not read. FSSAI caps Total Polar Compounds in frying oil at 25% and requires disposal records from kitchens using over 50 litres a day ([FSSAI RUCO booklet](https://eatrightindia.gov.in/ruco/file/ruco_booklet.pdf)). A Testo 270 oil tester costs about Rs 44,000 ([Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/product/testo-270-cooking-oil-tester/)). No small restaurant will buy one, but one meter carried by an ECCS technician can test every fryer on every visit. That is a place where a service company beats a software company.

**WhatsApp is the right channel for owners, but it waits its turn.** Utility messages cost about Rs 0.115 each in India against roughly Rs 0.86 for marketing messages, per reseller blogs rather than Meta's rate card ([Baat.ai](https://baat.ai/blog/whatsapp-api-pricing-india)). Keep every template transactional. Under the project's own rule, these go into `docs/NOTIFICATIONS.md` now and get built with the rest of the notifications later.

**The three planned features need refinements, not rethinking.** For labels, cheap 58 mm Bluetooth printers list at Rs 1,000 to 2,000 ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/proddetail/ally-vue-bluetooth-thermal-label-printer-heavy-duty-android-ios-2853775352930.html)), but the risk is the hardware: buy two models and test label mode, Indian scripts (print the label as an image) and whether labels survive a freezer before writing much code. Add an "expiring today" list, since expired stock is the most-cited raid finding. For the public QR page, the strong evidence comes from mandatory government schemes: Los Angeles grade cards lifted A-grade restaurants' revenue 5.7% ([Jin and Leslie](https://econ.umd.edu/sites/www.econ.umd.edu/files/pubs/ReputationAEJFinalVersion.pdf)) and about 41% of UK consumers check ratings ([FSA](https://www.food.gov.uk/research/food-and-you-2-fhrs-wave-4-executive-summary)). A voluntary score issued by the vendor paid to raise it will have a weaker effect, and no source measured private badges at all. So show the inspection date and a verifiable certificate number, show the inspected component separately from self-reported checklists, let the page lapse to "not current" automatically, publish the scoring method, and never resemble FSSAI's smileys. For attendance and salary, Petpooja Payroll already sells at Rs 7,000 in the first year including a biometric device and Rs 2,500 on renewal ([Techjockey](https://techjockey.com/detail/petpooja-pos)), so keep it thin and tie it to hygiene (who did which checklist, whose medical is due). The labour codes took effect on 21 November 2025 but Telangana's rules were still in draft ([Lakshmikumaran & Sridharan](https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/states/telangana)), so avoid hard-coding statutory wage logic.

## Certificates, rating readiness and partner-run tests add revenue without new licences

**Recommendation one: sell the FSSAI hygiene rating as a packaged outcome.** Of roughly 75,000 food establishments in Hyderabad, about 25,000 are licensed and **only 361 hold a hygiene rating**, the lowest among metros ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/hyderabad-ranks-lowest-food-hygiene-standards-among-major-cities-3493145/)). The rating checklist is built on Schedule 4 and "aligned with inspection checklist of regulatory staff", is valid two years, allows a re-audit after three months, and lets the restaurant choose its own recognised agency ([FSSAI guidance](https://hygiene.fssai.gov.in/resources/Guidance_Document_Hygiene_Rating.pdf)). ECCS should map its 92-point inspection to that checklist, add a "ready for rating audit" indicator, and sell a fixed-fee readiness package: gap assessment, cleaning and fixes, the compliance file, then an audit booked with a recognised agency. Effort is a few days of mapping plus one agency relationship. Two cautions. The low uptake also shows that voluntary certification has almost no pull by itself, so the pitch is "be ready when the task force walks in, and get the badge as proof", not the badge alone. And the scheme has stayed voluntary for seven years after FSSAI said in 2019 it would become mandatory ([Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/fssai-to-make-food-hygiene-ratings-mandatory-for-all-restaurants.n18828)); do not plan on that changing.

**Recommendation two: turn hood and duct cleaning into a schedule with a certificate.** No Indian rule fixing a duct-cleaning frequency was found, so the market runs on the American NFPA 96 intervals: monthly for solid-fuel cooking, quarterly for high-volume, half-yearly for moderate ([Oxmaint](https://oxmaint.com/industries/facility-management/commercial-kitchen-hood-exhaust-nfpa96-maintenance)). Tandoor and charcoal kitchens, common in Hyderabad, fall in the monthly band. A fire at Minerva Grand Hotel was attributed to oil residue in the chimneys ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/fire-breaks-out-at-hotel-in-hyderabad-third-incident-reported-in-24-hours-3241628/amp/)), and the Niloufer Cafe building lost its fire NOC after a surprise inspection ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/fire-noc-of-niloufer-cafe-building-cancelled-after-inspection-3487901/amp/)). Institutional buyers already specify the product: an IIM Lucknow tender budgets about Rs 2 lakh a year for quarterly hood-to-outlet cleaning with written reports for one kitchen ([IIM Lucknow](https://iiml.ac.in/sites/default/files/upload/tender/61284135TenderCleaningChimney.pdf)). The dated certificate, hood sticker and photo report live in the app's equipment register. Certificates must state which duct sections were reached and which were not, because ECCS carries liability if a fire follows a clean it certified. Extinguisher refilling and suppression-system servicing go to a licensed fire contractor on referral.

**Recommendation three: add partner-delivered compliance items to each visit.** Each of these adds a renewal date ECCS controls, and none needs ECCS to hold an accreditation.

| Service line | How ECCS delivers it | Price anchor in the notes | Verify first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water testing | Technician draws the sample; an approved lab reports; the report lands in the app | Rs 1,000 to 2,000 per test ([STIC tariff](https://sticindia.com/resources/Analysis%20Charges%20-%20from%2001-06-2025.pdf)) | Lab referral terms; whether NABL is required |
| Staff medical camps | Clinic partner on site; certificates uploaded with a 12-month reminder | Rs 1,350 per person at a Bengaluru chain ([RxDx](https://rxdx.in/fssai-food-handlers-health-checkup/)) | Hyderabad rates; which tests are actually required (sources conflict) |
| Supervisor (FoSTaC) training | ECCS fills batches; an empanelled partner trains and certifies | Rs 1,500 to 2,000 plus GST per seat ([FoSTaC](https://fostac.fssai.gov.in/advance.jsp)) | Partner's terms |
| Grease trap and silt chamber cleaning | Own crews, recurring | None reliable | Lawful disposal route for the waste |
| Tank cleaning | Add-on during a deep clean | Rs 800 to 1,200 per small tank, city unconfirmed ([ALP Consulting](https://dev.alp.consulting/?p=987766)) | Hyderabad quotes |
| Chemicals and tools refilled on the visit | Distributor account; labelled dispensers and dilution charts | None found | Distributor margins and minimums |

Training is a retention line, not a profit line; ECCS does not fit the listed training-partner categories. Its unaccredited edge is in-kitchen coaching in Telugu, Hindi and Urdu during visits, priced into plan tiers. Grease traps have real enforcement behind them: the Hyderabad water board ran a drive ordering hotels and bakeries to install silt chambers and fined a hotel Rs 10,000 for dumping waste into a manhole ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/hyderabad-hotel-fined-rs-10k-for-dumping-waste-after-malakpet-sewer-inspection-3228674/amp/)).

**A timely but narrower opening is waste compliance.** On 30 September 2026, Hyderabad's civic bodies warned bulk waste generators, hotels included, of power or water disconnection; Malkajgiri alone counted 570 of 986 identified generators as unregistered ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/telangana/civic-bodies-warn-bwgs-of-power-cuts-for-non-compliance-1992052)). The threshold is 100 kg of waste a day per exam-prep summaries of the 2026 rules ([Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/solid-waste-management-rules-2026/)), so this touches large restaurants, hotels and food courts only. Helping them register and connect to an authorised processor is a door-opener through partners, not a core line.

**On pest control, check the licence position and sell the paperwork.** A separate licence under the Insecticides Rules covers stocking and using restricted insecticides for commercial pest control and requires a qualified graduate with recognised training ([Form VI-A](https://www.meghalaya.gov.in/meghalaya/sites/default/files/forms/commercial_pest_control.pdf)). The researchers read this as triggered by fumigants, not by gel baits and traps; confirm with the Telangana Agriculture Department before any restricted product is used. Rentokil PCI already gives clients a portal with pest activity logged against each barcoded station ([Rentokil PCI](https://www.rentokil-pestcontrolindia.com/integrated-pest-management/myrentokil-pci/)). ECCS's app should match that record: a station map, visit reports, the chemical list with safety sheets, and a sighting log.

## Bundle the app into service plans and sell to clusters first

**Price the service and include the software.** A vendor blog puts repairs and maintenance at 1 to 2% of revenue and technology at 1 to 2% ([DineOpen](https://www.dineopen.com/blog/restaurant-profit-margins-india-guide.html)); for an outlet turning over Rs 10 lakh a month, that is Rs 10,000 to 20,000 a month for all maintenance, and the same again for all software. The source is a marketer, so treat it as a rough envelope. The pitch is consolidation: restaurants pay four or more vendors today for pest control, duct cleaning, audits and software, and none of the competitors found offers one plan. Two design points follow from sourced facts. UPI AutoPay mandates default to a **Rs 15,000 cap per transaction**, and PhonePe and BHIM support only up to that amount ([Razorpay docs](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/supported-banks-apps.md)), so monthly plans above Rs 15,000 need invoice-and-pay or card mandates. And Razorpay charges 2% plus GST on its fee ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)), so offer bank transfer for larger invoices. Because small outlets churn heavily (25 to 30% of cloud kitchens are said to close in year one, per a [Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/why-cloud-kitchens-shutting-down/)), favour quarterly or annual prepayment for independents.

**Target buyers who cover many kitchens with one decision.** The notes' reasoning, which no source describes directly, points to four segments. Regional chains and franchisees need the same evidenced standard across outlets and are being raided: the task force inspected 55 pizza outlets statewide, including 18 Pizza Hut and 16 Domino's ([Deccan Chronicle](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/amp/telangana-pizza-hut-dominos-under-food-safety-radar)). Cloud-kitchen operators have dense sites and live on aggregator listings. Contract caterers serving IT parks and hospitals are audited by their own clients. Mall food courts are inspected as a unit, with tenants individually served notices ([Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/business/fssai-task-force-uncovers-violations-at-banjara-hills-mall-eateries-891660)), which makes the landlord a plausible single buyer. A read-only web view for a chain's head office would be the one feature these buyers need that the app lacks. Small independents buy after a problem; serve them with a published rate card and prepaid packs. Commercial pricing in India is quoted by site visit, so a transparent rate card is itself a differentiator.

**Three partnerships have evidence behind them.** First, an FSSAI-recognised audit agency: Equinox publishes its fees and is named by Swiggy as an audit partner ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/technology/news/story/swiggys-new-seal-badge-explained-food-delivery-platform-introduces-new-program-to-maintain-hygiene-451293-2024-10-23)); the agency audits, ECCS prepares and fixes. Second, the aggregators: Swiggy said partners would get exclusive pricing on cleaning and pest control through its Owner app ([Swiggy blog](https://blog.swiggy.com/?p=22272)), and Zomato's Restaurant Services Hub works through third-party vendors ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2024/06/zomato-launches-restaurant-services-hub-qsrs)). Whether either programme is live in Hyderabad and how vendors join was not confirmed, and the risk is becoming a discounted commodity vendor. Third, the NRAI Hyderabad chapter, which launched in 2022 with about 250 members ([BW Hotelier](https://bwhotelier.businessworld.in:443/article/Hyderabad-Chapter-of-National-Restaurant-Association-of-India-launched/29-01-2022-419122)); small, but it concentrates the organised owners. Dasha Food Safety deserves one conversation as either a competitor or a partner, since it has consultant credibility and no software.

**Cheap credentials open institutional kitchens.** Udyam (MSME) and GeM registration bring earnest-money exemption and procurement preference, per consultant guides ([IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/gem-registration-sell-to-government-india)), though the IIM Lucknow tender shows buyers still demand turnover in the specific work and references from three-star-plus hotels. Two or three hotel reference letters are worth more than any certificate. Sales copy should lead with closure risk and the 2026 raids. Quote the Los Angeles 5.7% figure with its caveat; Zomato's claim that hygiene-rated restaurants "fared 20% better" is the company's own, from 2020 ([The Ken](https://the-ken.com/story/when-in-a-pandemic-sell-hygiene/)).

## What to refuse, and what a dozen phone calls must settle

**Do not build hardware or camera products.** Continuous fridge sensors list at Rs 4,500 to 10,000 each before a gateway ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/impcat/wireless-sensors.html)), adoption is claimed at under 5% of restaurants even in rich markets ([QSR Magazine](https://www.qsrmagazine.com/uncategorized/why-temperature-sensors-are-worthy-investment/)), and Bluetooth hardware is a recurring complaint about Zenput ([G2](https://g2.com/products/zenput/reviews?page=2)). CCTV analytics has a funded Indian incumbent in Wobot ([YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2020/08/funding-wobot-pre-series-a-sequoia-capital)), and the best published accuracy found was 85.7% on a curated dataset ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12526732/)), which is not field accuracy in a steamy kitchen; refer customers who ask. Live kitchen feeds for diners have no demand evidence and real privacy exposure, with India's data protection rules taking full effect in May 2027 ([Sansa Legal](https://www.sansalegal.com/post/india-s-digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-a-business-compliance-guide)). Machine-learning prediction of inspection failure has no data to learn from at ECCS's scale. Fully voice-driven checklists rest on mixed evidence from outside kitchens; a voice note on a failed item is the cheap substitute. A Petpooja integration waits until a chain demands it, since its known APIs carry menus and orders ([APIs.io](https://apis.io/providers/petpooja/)). One optional pilot is defensible after the photo-integrity basics: a low-cost vision model that asks "is this a blurred photo, or a photo of a screen?" and routes doubtful ones to the office board, at an assumption-based few dollars a month. It must never change a score by itself.

**Do not take on roles that compromise the score or need heavy licences.** Becoming a recognised hygiene rating agency means Quality Council of India recognition and puts ECCS in the position of officially rating kitchens it is paid to clean. Collecting used cooking oil needs certification through a biodiesel maker ([FSSAI RUCO booklet](https://eatrightindia.gov.in/ruco/file/ruco_booklet.pdf)); log and refer instead. Fire NOC work should stop at a readiness checklist and never promise clearance. Generic manpower contracts earn 5 to 7% EBITDA even at scale (SIS's facility-management segment made 5.5% in Q4 FY26, per its [earnings update](https://sisindia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Earning-Updates.pdf)), so stay in specialised, certificated work. Do not sell the app as a standalone subscription to independents, and do not sell licence-filing as a business: fees for FSSAI's certified intermediaries are capped at Rs 100 to 500 ([Velco Legal](https://velcolegalindia.com/blog/fssai-license-fees-complete-guide-2026)), and whether restaurants must file the annual return at all is disputed between sources.

**Do not make claims the evidence cannot carry.** No Indian insurer wording was found that requires duct-cleaning certificates or discounts premiums for them; the only Indian assertion is a marketing blog. Sell the certificate as evidence for fire inspections and claim disputes. Do not claim the QR page lifts sales. Do not ask only happy customers for Google reviews or offer incentives; Google prohibits incentivised reviews ([Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en-GB)).

**The gaps are large and mostly local.** The research found no reliable Hyderabad price for commercial pest-control contracts, hood and duct cleaning (one IndiaMART listing says Rs 3 per sq ft, another supplier Rs 200 to 250 per sq m), grease-trap cleaning, commercial tank cleaning, or Dasha's retainer. These need phone quotes from Rentokil PCI, HiCare and local operators, and five to ten customer interviews on what restaurants pay now. Also unverified: whether Swiggy's and Zomato's vendor programmes run in Hyderabad; which recognised audit agencies operate there; lab and clinic partner terms; the aggregators' current treatment of hygiene ratings and lapsed licences; whether GHMC has been split and which corporation licenses a given address; the fire NOC renewal period (sources say both annual and about three years); the Gazette text of the 2026 FSSAI amendments; and Meta's and Razorpay's actual rate cards. The Hyderabad competitor list is incomplete, and Petpooja Tasks' price and offline behaviour are unknown. Most compliance facts here come from explainers and search summaries, not primary documents, so check any rule against FSSAI's own text before printing it in sales material or building a reminder around it.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. ECCS set out asking what features to add, and the honest answer is that the app already has more breadth than its market will pay for by itself, while a much larger rival covers the checklist half. What no rival has is a person who visits, an office that watches the data, and a record that stands up when eight inspection teams and the police arrive unannounced. Every recommendation above strengthens that chain: a score that cannot easily be faked, a file with the records inspectors cite, instruments a restaurant would never buy, and an official badge that only a few hundred Hyderabad kitchens hold.

The less comfortable implication is that ECCS's growth is limited by service capacity and local trust, not by code. The sensible order is therefore the phone calls and customer interviews first, the eight to nine weeks of integrity and compliance-file work alongside them, then one rating-readiness pilot with one audit agency and one multi-outlet customer. If that pilot produces a kitchen that passes an official audit and a customer who renews, ECCS has a product no competitor in these notes can copy quickly. If restaurants will not pay for readiness even in a year of public closures, no further feature would have changed that.
