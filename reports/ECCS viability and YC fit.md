# Win paying kitchens before courting YC

ECCS is viable as a specialised hygiene-services company for Hyderabad kitchens, with the app as its proof of work, but only if three things that nobody has yet tested turn out to be true: restaurants pay something near Rs 6,000 a month and renew, crews are kept busy inside a few dense neighbourhoods, and the work stays specialised (pest control, duct cleaning, scored inspections) instead of drifting into supplying general cleaning labour. It is not, on today's evidence, a venture-scale business, meaning one that can grow large enough to repay investors who need a very big outcome: Hyderabad alone is worth somewhere between Rs 5 crore and Rs 150 crore a year on the researchers' own arithmetic, the investors backing "services" in 2026 are backing desk work that AI can automate, and an app built in a week is an app a rival can copy in a week. Abroad, the cleaning cannot be exported at all; only the Gulf has rules that fit the bundle, and only through a locally licensed partner once Hyderabad works. The founder should not apply to Y Combinator in earnest for the Winter 2027 batch (deadline 2 November 2026): acceptance is very unlikely for a solo founder who is not an engineer, with an India-only, labour-heavy business that no customer has used, and an acceptance would bring a foreign parent company, a stake in the mid-teens of percent and three months in San Francisco. If the founder wants a profitable local business, YC is the wrong tool at any date; if the founder wants venture scale, the time to apply is 2027, with paying customers, renewal figures and preferably a co-founder. Three facts missing from the project's records (whether a cleaning business with customers already exists, the founder's history in the trade, and which of those two aims the founder holds) each move this verdict, and the report shows how. A warning on evidence: every researcher ran out of web searches part-way through, no price actually paid by a Hyderabad restaurant was found, and the margin, market-size and odds figures below are the researchers' own estimates and are labelled that way.

## Raids now shut one kitchen in four they visit, yet only a few hundred kitchens buy hygiene on a schedule

**Hyderabad has about 75,000 food outlets, of which about 25,000 held a full FSSAI licence and 361 held a hygiene rating in June 2026** ([MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/nrai-releases-india-food-services-report-2024/); [Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/hyderabad-ranks-lowest-food-hygiene-standards-among-major-cities-3493145/)). The first number is solid, because an industry report and a press report of FSSAI data agree. The other two rest on one press report. Until April 2026 a licence, as opposed to basic registration, meant turnover above Rs 12 lakh a year, so the researcher treats the 25,000 as the widest sensible pool of outlets able to pay for any recurring service and the other 50,000 as outside the market for a subscription. That yardstick has since broken: FSSAI raised its turnover limits from 1 April 2026, seen only as a search listing of a legal explainer ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/food-and-drugs-law/1801924/fssai-raises-turnover-limits-for-food-business-registration-and-licensing)), and no count on the new basis exists.

What changed in 2026 is the kind of enforcement. Press reports of six drives in September 2026 add up to **140 establishments inspected and 37 licences suspended, 26% of those visited**, with police present and orders to stop trading at once ([Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/cockroaches-rat-faeces-found-as-tg-safe-acts-against-11-hyderabad-eateries); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/telangana/hyderabad-food-safety-crackdown-five-fssai-licences-suspended-in-kukatpally/); [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/tg-safe-suspends-fssai-licences-of-nine-food-establishments-in-lb-nagar)). For comparison, the state's food safety commissioner reported four suspensions from 4,366 inspections over roughly nine months of 2024 ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/over-4k-food-safety-raids-target-restaurants-in-hyderabad-fb-sector-3151580/amp/)). Fines hardly figure: Telangana recorded no penalty and no conviction in 2025-26 against 398 failed samples, with 44 of 81 food safety officer posts filled ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/398-telangana-food-samples-fail-tests-but-0-penalties-convictions-3521827/)). The researcher's arithmetic puts the chance of a raid at about 0.6% of licensed outlets a month, roughly 7% a year at September's pace. So the honest sales message is "if they come, one in four is shut that day, and they ask for your records", and a message built on "you will be raided" overstates the odds. Inspectors now cite paperwork beside the dirt, including "inadequate pest management controls" and "inadequate record keeping" in the reports above. A second driver is commercial: in July 2026 the Cyberabad Municipal Corporation told Swiggy and Zomato to list only outlets with valid FSSAI and trade licences and to remove those whose licences had expired or been suspended ([Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/news/cyberabad-orders-swiggy-and-zomato-to-remove-unlicensed-restaurants-and-cloud-kitchens.n16744)), which puts most of a delivery kitchen's revenue at stake. Fire is a weak driver, since no Indian rule fixing how often a kitchen duct must be cleaned was found ([Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/guides/kitchen-hood-suppression-systems-india)). No October drives were retrieved, so whether September's pace has continued is unknown.

The demand that is proven is small. The 361 rated outlets, and one Hyderabad consultant's claim of 200-plus kitchens on a monthly compliance retainer ([Dasha Food Safety](https://dashafoodsafety.com/)), are the only numbers found that describe restaurants already paying for ongoing hygiene; the researcher reads them as a group numbering in the hundreds, not the thousands. No source measures how many restaurants hold a yearly contract for pest control or cleaning, which the researcher calls the single most important missing number. No source states what a restaurant actually pays, either. Only marketplace listing prices exist: deep cleaning at Rs 4 to Rs 14 per square foot ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/hyderabad/deep-cleaning-services.html)), pest control at Rs 1 to Rs 6 per square foot ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/hyderabad/pest-control-services.html)) and duct cleaning anywhere from Rs 100 to Rs 3,000 per square metre ([IndiaMART](https://m.indiamart.com/city/hyderabad/duct-cleaning-services.html)), a thirty-fold spread that shows the listings cannot be compared. From those listings the researcher's sums give a 1,500 sq ft restaurant that bought every service on a proper schedule a yearly bill of very roughly Rs 55,000 to Rs 2.3 lakh, and a small cloud kitchen Rs 26,000 to Rs 52,000. Restaurants have little room for it: Redseer puts operating margins at roughly 8% for dine-in and 12% for cloud kitchens ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/cloud-kitchens-vs-dine-in-restaurants-which-business-model-is-making-more-money-540655-2026-07-03)), so a Rs 10,000-a-month plan is about one eighth of the monthly profit of an outlet turning over Rs 10 lakh (researcher's arithmetic). That explains why small independents buy after a scare and not on a calendar. They also close often: a vendor blog quoting a trade source says 25 to 30% of cloud kitchens shut within a year ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/why-cloud-kitchens-shutting-down/)), and no body in India publishes a measured restaurant failure rate ([Petpooja](https://blog.petpooja.com/?p=23372)).

The market size that follows is the researcher's arithmetic on two inputs that have no measured source. The shares of outlets that would hold a plan are assumptions; only the low case stays near behaviour anyone has observed.

| Case | Outlets holding a plan (share of 25,000) | Yearly spend per outlet | Hyderabad, per year | All India, per year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | 1,250 (5%) | Rs 40,000 | Rs 5 crore | about Rs 120 crore |
| Middle | 3,000 (12%) | Rs 1.2 lakh | Rs 36 crore | about Rs 880 crore |
| High | 6,250 (25%) | Rs 2.4 lakh | Rs 150 crore | about Rs 3,700 crore |

The all-India column multiplies Hyderabad by about 24.5, which is the ratio of India's organised food-service market to Hyderabad's Rs 10,161 crore ([Restaurant India](https://www.restaurantindia.in/article/india-to-be-the-3rd-largest-food-service-market-by-2028-overtaking-japan-nrai-ifsr-2024); [MediaBrief](https://mediabrief.com/nrai-releases-india-food-services-report-2024/)); it assumes other cities enforce and pay as Hyderabad does, which is unproven. What the numbers mean for one company is plainer. At the middle spend, **Rs 1 crore of yearly revenue needs about 83 outlets and Rs 10 crore needs about 833, which is 28% of the middle-case base in the whole city**. Hyderabad alone supports a sound local services business on these figures; anything larger needs several cities or far higher spend per customer than the listings suggest. For a sense of scale, India's second-largest pest-control company, HiCare, had reached about Rs 80 crore of yearly revenue across homes and businesses nationwide when Rentokil PCI bought it in 2024 ([Rentokil Initial](https://www.rentokil-initial.com/media/news-releases/2024/india.aspx)). The likeliest early customers are operators who cover many kitchens with one decision (regional chains, cloud-kitchen brands, caterers) and restaurants of 50 or more seats, which have had named obligations for medicals, water tests and pest logs since October 2024 ([Deccan Chronicle](https://deccanchronicle.com/news/hyderabad-mandates-cctv-in-kitchens-for-food-safety-1828317)). No count of any of these groups in Hyderabad was found.

## Every service has a seller, nobody sells the bundle, and about 80 outlets fill one crew

Each piece of what ECCS offers already has an established seller in Hyderabad, and each is sold separately. Pest control belongs to Rentokil PCI, which bought HiCare in April 2024 and whose chief executive counts "over 6,000 small pest control companies in India" ([Rentokil Initial](https://www.rentokil-initial.com/media/news-releases/2024/india.aspx)); local operators sell restaurants a monthly contract with a visit report "ready to file in your register" ([Deccan Pest Control](https://www.deccanpestcontrol.com/restaurant-cloud-kitchen-pest-control-hyderabad/)). About two dozen vendors list duct cleaning in the city ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/hyderabad/duct-cleaning-services.html)), and one kitchen-equipment firm publishes maintenance contracts at Rs 25,000 to Rs 45,000 a month ([My Kitchen Experts](https://mykitchenexperts.in/)). Official hygiene-rating audits cost Rs 9,500 to Rs 15,000 ([Equinox Labs](https://equinoxlab.com/hygiene-rating-audit-fssai.php)). Dasha Food Safety sells the nearest thing to ECCS's plan: a monthly retainer with weekly inspections, a tracking dashboard and named clients such as Pista House, but no cleaning or pest crews ([Dasha Food Safety](https://dashafoodsafety.com/)). Checklists with photo proof and AI checking already ship inside Petpooja, the billing software used by more than 100,000 restaurants, where "5,000+ businesses" use them ([Petpooja blog](https://blog.petpooja.com/operations-workflows/how-petpooja-tasks-work/)). The delivery platforms own the badge diners see and the vendor marketplace: Swiggy awards its Seal from customer feedback, and Zomato's services hub reached 3,200 restaurants in a six-month trial ([Verdict Foodservice](https://www.verdictfoodservice.com/news/swiggy-seal-650-indian-cities/); [Verdict Foodservice](https://www.verdictfoodservice.com/news/zomato-restaurant-services-hub)).

**No company was found, alive or dead, that sells recurring cleaning, pest control and scored inspection together with an app the restaurant's own staff open every day.** The search was cut short, so this is "not found", not "does not exist". The researcher offers two readings and cannot choose between them: the bundle needs both crews and software and firms have one or the other, or the firms with most to gain have each decided restaurants buy these things from different budgets. Only customer interviews can tell. What the evidence does settle is that software sold by itself earns little from Indian restaurants. Petpooja's Rs 76 crore of revenue from more than 100,000 clients is at most about Rs 7,600 per client a year by the researcher's arithmetic, after thirteen years and still at a loss ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/petpooja-raises-155-mn-series-c-at-over-3x-valuation-premium-10512396)). The attendance and salary features in ECCS's app should be judged the same way, as reasons for a customer to stay and not as something to charge for.

The comparable companies show that margin in this trade follows specialisation and repeat visits, not the presence of software. "Operating margin" below means profit before interest, tax and depreciation as a share of sales.

| Kind of business | Example | What it earns |
|---|---|---|
| General cleaning and facility manpower | Updater Services; Krystal Integrated | 5.2% and 6.5% operating margin in FY26 ([Multibagg](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/uds-fy26-95310); [FilingReader](https://filingreader.com/news-wire/mumbai/2026-05-08/krystal-integrated-services-revenue-hits-rs-1277-crore-in-fy26)) |

| Low-priced, discounted cleaning | Urban Company's InstaHelp | A loss of Rs 346 on every order ([Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/stocks/urban-company-q1-fy-2026-27-results-revenue-up-shares-jump-despite-loss/)) |

Labour became dearer and more certain this year. From 1 June 2026 the legal minimum monthly wage in Hyderabad is **Rs 16,000 for a sweeper and Rs 18,500 for a pest-control operator** ([G.O.Ms.No.6](https://unitedconsultancy.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Telangana-New-Minimum-Wages-GO-No-6-Date-30-May-2026.pdf)). Adding provident fund, state insurance, bonus and gratuity, the researcher puts the employer's cost at about Rs 20,700 and Rs 23,900. A compliant operator therefore pays more than the unregistered crews it quotes against. The same wage order makes the customer responsible when a contractor underpays, so proof of lawful wages is something to sell to chains. On assumed crew sizes, hours and materials, none of which has a source, the researcher estimates what is left of the price after paying for the crew, chemicals and travel (the gross margin):

| Job or plan | Assumed price | Direct cost | Gross margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Deep clean of a 1,500 sq ft kitchen, one night, three cleaners and a supervisor | Rs 8,000 | Rs 5,621 | 30% (6% at Rs 6,000; 46% at Rs 10,500) |
| Hood and duct clean, one shift | Rs 8,500 (ECCS's sample price) | Rs 5,355 | 37% |
| Monthly pest visit, one operator doing four a day | Rs 1,500 | Rs 680 | 55% (39% at two a day) |
| Monthly plan: pest visit and inspection monthly, deep clean quarterly, duct clean half-yearly, app included | Rs 6,000 | Rs 4,145 | 31% |

These figures are estimates and they swing hardest on idle time. If a salaried deep-clean crew works only half its nights, the Rs 8,000 job loses about Rs 1,140. On the same assumptions **about 80 subscribed outlets fill one full team of roughly eight people, and break-even with Rs 1.5 lakh a month of assumed overheads is about 81 outlets**. The planned pilot of 15 to 20 restaurants uses about a quarter of one team's time, and a salaried five-person team costs about Rs 1.15 lakh a month against pilot revenue of Rs 1.2 lakh at 20 outlets; the pilot loses money on labour unless staff are multi-skilled and partly paid per job, or one-off jobs fill the calendar. Crews are hard to keep: a Deloitte study of Indian blue-collar work found 69% of hires temporary and an average stay of 21 months ([Deloitte India](https://www.deloitte.com/in/en/services/consulting/services/human-capital/blue-collar-workforce-trends-2025.html)), and Snabbit, a funded home-help company with more than 15,000 workers, now recruits cleaners in Hyderabad ([Open Magazine](https://openthemagazine.com/business/bigg-boss-big-losses-snabbit-is-spending-like-a-giant-on-11-cr-revenue)). The researcher's reading of what protects a newcomer is short: customers packed into a few areas, a year of dated records for each kitchen that a new vendor cannot recreate, the same crew and inspector on the same customer, lawful employment, and two or three recognised local names as references. The app's features, the pest licence, a private badge and low price protect nothing.

## The cleaning cannot leave India, and only the Gulf's rules fit the bundle

Abroad, the money is in service routes and not in apps. Ecolab's division selling cleaning programmes to restaurants and hotels took US$6.10 billion in 2025 at a 22.7% operating margin, and its commercial pest division US$1.25 billion at 19.5% ([Ecolab](https://dotcom-www.ecolab.com/media-center/news/ecolab-announces-record-fourth-quarter-and-strong-2026-outlook-reported-diluted-eps-1-98-adjusted-diluted-eps-2-08-15-2026-adjusted-diluted-eps-outlook-8-43--8-63-12--15)); Rentokil's pest control took US$5.7 billion ([Rentokil Initial](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/rentokil-initial--rto/preliminary-results/9459207)). The named food-safety software companies are tiny by comparison. Jolt, a US restaurant checklist product, had "over $20 million" of yearly subscription revenue when it was sold for about **US$145.5 million** in August 2025 ([Digi International 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/854775/000085477525000022/digiinternationalacquiresj.htm)), and Britain's Checkit, on £13.7 million of revenue, put itself up for sale in March 2026 ([Checkit](https://www.investegate.co.uk/announcement/rns/checkit--ckt/final-results/9529267)). Hood cleaning itself is a trade of small local firms everywhere figures were found: estimates of the US market run from about US$0.4 billion to US$2 billion, the largest network has 135 units averaging US$577,400 each, and the median US hood-cleaning business offered for sale turns over about US$655,000 and asks 0.72 times its yearly revenue ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/?p=67541); [Franzy](https://franzy.com/franchises/hoodz)).

Nobody abroad sells ECCS's bundle either. Every software vendor found sells software alone, sometimes with sensors; the nearest "software plus people" offer is advisory, such as Britain's Food Alert, which pairs apps with consultants and audits and does no cleaning ([Food Alert](https://www.foodalert.com/food-alert-steps-up-growth-with-latest-acquisition/)). An adviser's tracker says no investor-led consolidation of hood-cleaning operators exists in the record through October 2026 ([CT Acquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/?p=67541)). The closest foreign precedent is Steritech, which sold commercial pest control and food-safety audits to restaurants and was bought by Rentokil in 2015 for US$425 million, 2.8 times its revenue ([Potomac Company](https://potomaccompany.com/transactions/pest-control-transaction-multiples-peak-as-rentokil-initial-acquires-the-steritech-group-for-425-million/)). That shows the business is real and saleable; it was built in North America with chain customers and without venture funding. The researcher's caution applies abroad as at home: an open field is also a field where nobody has shown that restaurants want one vendor for everything.

The service does not export, because the work must be done by a locally licensed company with locally approved staff. In Dubai a pest-control firm needs a trade licence and a separate Municipality permit, both renewed yearly, and every technician needs certified training and a UAE residence visa, according to a free-zone operator's guide ([Meydan Free Zone](https://www.meydanfz.ae/activity-hub/apply-for-a-public-health-pests-control-services-license-in-dubai)). Singapore caps foreign workers at 35% of a services firm's staff and admits Indian workers only for a restricted set of occupations ([Ministry of Manpower](https://www.mom.gov.sg/passes-and-permits/work-permit-for-foreign-worker/sector-specific-rules/services-sector-requirements)). Britain's Skilled Worker visa needs a salary of at least £41,700 ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/skilled-worker-visa/your-job)). In Singapore, Britain and the United States the realistic export is therefore software, into markets where it already has a price (FoodDocs lists at US$99 to US$299 per site per month ([FoodDocs](https://www.fooddocs.com/pricing))) and where contractors already run on established tools; more than 800 US service contractors use one of them ([Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/commercial-service-contractor-platform-servicetrade-raises-85-million)).

The Gulf is the exception. Dubai requires a certified "Person in Charge" at every food establishment, filing daily checklist reports to the Municipality's own platform; it oversaw 72,108 of them in 2020 ([Dubai Municipality](https://www.dm.gov.ae/dubai-municipality-oversees-more-than-70000-mandatory-pics-in-dubais-food-establishments/)). Singapore's new grading scheme, in force since January 2026 for about 45,000 establishments, will make restaurants that want the top grade keep a food-safety system with cleaning, pest-control and record-keeping ([Singapore Food Agency](https://www.sfa.gov.sg/news-publications/newsroom/new-safety-assurance-for-food-establishment--safe--framework--to-strengthen-food-safety)), and Britain's duct-cleaning standard demands a dated, photographed report after every clean ([BESA](https://www.thebesa.com/besa-blogs/kitchen-extract-system-cleaning)). These are the records ECCS's app produces as a by-product of its visits. The catch in Dubai is that the regulator already owns the daily-record channel, and whether an outside app can feed it is unknown. The nearest Indian precedent, Urban Company, needed until late 2025 for its UAE and Singapore business to reach a small profit, Rs 4 crore on Rs 50 crore of quarterly revenue ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/urban-company-q3-fy26-revenue-jumps-to-383-cr-losses-persist-amid-instahelp-expansion)). The researcher's credible order is Hyderabad, then two or three Indian cities, then Dubai through a licensed partner, with software-only sales elsewhere as an option and not a plan. The one cheap step today is to keep the app's checklists, score weights and licence types adjustable by country. This section is thin on the Gulf's economics: no prices, labour costs or demand evidence were found for any Gulf market, and Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Malaysia were barely covered.

## Investors are funding desk work that AI replaces, not duct cleaning

The 2026 enthusiasm for "services" is real, well funded and about work done at a desk. Sequoia's March 2026 essay argues that "for every dollar spent on software, six are spent on services" and lists insurance broking, accounting, legal work, IT support and recruitment as targets, with no physical work among them ([Sequoia](https://sequoiacap.com/article/services-the-new-software/)). General Catalyst set aside US$1.5 billion to buy service firms and automate them ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/28/the-ai-services-transformation-may-be-harder-than-vcs-think/)), and its partner said on record that "plumbing and roofing and HVAC and a lot of like in person work" will not meet the firm's test of 30% of tasks automated, "at least not right now" ([Cognitive Revolution](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-revolution-in-services-general-catalysts-ai-rollup-strategy-with-marc-bhargava/)). The researcher's reading is that half of this thinking fits ECCS and half does not. Restaurants already pay outsiders for pest control, duct cleaning and audits, which is Sequoia's own test of where to start. But the promise of software-like profit rests on AI doing most of the work, and nobody automates scrubbing a duct. The nearest fundable wording in this year's vocabulary is YC's Fall 2026 request for "new operating systems for the physical world", software that directs field work and records "all the work as it actually happens" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/rfs)). That request is about the system and its data, and an investor would still ask why the company must own the crews.

The record of venture-backed companies that deliver a physical service is mixed, and worst for sales to businesses. Homejoy raised over US$38 million, spread to more than 31 cities and shut in July 2015 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homejoy)); a Harvard Business School student analysis found 75% of its bookings came from discounts and fewer than 10% of customers were still using it after six months ([HBS Digital Initiative](https://d3.harvard.edu/platform-digit/submission/homejoy-a-silicon-valley-darlings-path-to-the-grave/)). Managed by Q, which employed its own office cleaners, was bought by WeWork for US$220 million in 2019 and resold eleven months later for **US$25 million** ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/office/eden-buys-managed-by-q-from-wework-for-fire-sale-25m-price-103254)). The one Indian services company that became venture-scale, Urban Company, was founded in 2014 and listed in September 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Company)). In 2026 Indian venture money for physical services went to consumer home help with very frequent orders: Snabbit raised US$56 million at about US$350 million and Pronto US$45 million at US$200 million ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/27/indias-snabbit-closes-56m-round-as-investor-interest-heats-up-in-on-demand-home-services/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/pronto-closes-series-b-round-at-45-mn-valuation-jumps-to-200-mn/)). No 2025-2026 funding round for an Indian startup selling cleaning, pest control or kitchen hygiene to businesses was found, and no investor has published a case for the category. That absence comes from a search that was cut short, and no Indian fund's own view could be retrieved.

How such a company is valued depends on which kind of service it turns out to be. On 12 October 2026 the Indian facility-management companies SIS and Updater traded at about 0.39 and 0.35 times yearly revenue with operating margins of 4 to 6%, while the US pest-control company Rollins traded at about 4.2 times revenue with a 22% margin ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/SIS/statistics/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/nse/UDS/statistics/); [stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/rol/statistics/)). These come from a share-data site and were not checked against company filings. A US broker's guide says small pest-control firms with mostly contracted customers sell for 1.5 to 2.5 times revenue, and that buyers pay most when more than three-quarters of revenue recurs and 85 to 92% of customers stay each year ([CT Acquisitions](https://www.ctacquisitions.com/how-to-sell-pest-control-business/)). No Indian sale prices were found. The size test is the researcher's own arithmetic: taking about US$100 million of yearly revenue as the mark of venture scale, which is about Rs 945 crore, ECCS would need about **94,500 outlets at Rs 1 lakh a year each**, nearly four times Hyderabad's licensed establishments. HiCare, selling pest control to every kind of customer nationwide, reached under a tenth of that. Venture scale needs a national footprint, much larger customers, or both.

Two further points bear on any pitch. First, investors now say openly that building software has become cheap. Blume Ventures wrote in October 2026 that "the cost of building has fallen, and a small team can now put together something that would once have taken months" ([Blume](https://blume.vc/commentaries/how-to-get-from-an-ai-demo-to-an-enterprise-customer-the-95-problem)). An app built in a week is proof of effort, and the speed of the build is itself evidence that a competitor could match it; what could count as an asset is what accumulates once real kitchens use it, and none of that exists yet. Second, the seed market is harder than it was. One dataset shows Indian seed-stage funding down 37% and first-time-funded companies down 30% in January to September 2026 ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/india-tech-funding-rises-7-to-103-billion-in-jan-sep-2026)); another shows seed funding up 18% in the first half with a median cheque of US$1 million ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/indian-startup-funding-slips-9-to-5-2-bn-in-h1-2026/)). The two use different data and periods; the second also reports AI funding up 317% in the same half-year, which is where the money is going.

The alternative to venture money deserves equal weight. As an illustration only, the researcher works out that a specialised hygiene firm with Rs 5 crore of revenue at a 12 to 20% operating margin would earn its owner Rs 60 lakh to Rs 1 crore a year before tax, with the founder owning all of it, and a named buyer exists in Rentokil PCI. The project's own records put the software's running cost at about Rs 23,500 to Rs 25,000 in the first year and Rs 2,300 to Rs 3,000 a month after, so the software needs no outside money. What equity would buy is speed, and nothing in the evidence shows a rival racing for the same restaurants.

## One hard licence, three dangers to design out, and a score that cannot call itself independent

The first risk is the plainest: the project's records say ECCS is not yet a registered company, and that blocks every licence, insurance policy, GST invoice, written contract and organisation app-store account. Beyond that, pest control is the one service with a real licensing gate. The Insecticides Rules require a licence from the state agriculture department, held by a graduate in agriculture or in science with chemistry who has completed 15 days' training at one of three institutes, one of them in Hyderabad ([Insecticides Rules 1971](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f130f9fca195c05cd02c7)). The two researchers read its reach differently: one reads the rule as tied to named fumigants, the other reads the Act's words "any insecticide" as covering gel-and-spray work too. Both arrive at the same practice, which is to subcontract pest control to a licensed operator in the first year, or hire a qualified person, and to ask the Commissioner of Agriculture's office in Basheerbagh for the position in writing. Buyers are already being told to ask vendors for the licence. No licence specific to cleaning, duct work or inspections was found; those need the ordinary set of a legal entity, Shops and Establishments registration, a trade licence, and GST once yearly turnover passes Rs 20 lakh ([ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/gst-registration-limits-increased)).

Employing crews lawfully adds roughly a quarter to a third to the wage bill by the researchers' sums, and India's four national Labour Codes have been in force since 21 November 2025 while Telangana's own rules were still drafts in mid-2026 ([factoHR](https://factohr.com/occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-code/); [Simpliance](https://simpliance.in/India/LEI/govt_notification/telangana/draft-notification-of-the-telangana-occupational-safety-health-and-working-conditions-rules-2026-9764)). A small team may sit below the head-counts at which provident fund and state insurance apply, but then the whole cost of an accident falls on ECCS: one guide's worked example for a 30-year-old on Rs 18,000 a month who dies at work is about **Rs 15.6 lakh** ([TaxGarden](https://taxgarden.in/blog/employees-compensation-act-1923-employer-guide-workplace-injury-compensation-india-2026)). An employees' compensation policy is needed from the first hire. Calling crews "gig" workers does not remove that duty, and Telangana's 2026 gig-workers law adds a welfare fee of 1 to 2% of each transaction for platforms it covers ([King Stubb & Kasiva](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/telangana-legislative-assembly-passed-telangana-platform-based-gig-workers-registration-social-security-and-welfare-act-2026-passed-on-march-29-2026/)); whether ECCS's own dispatch console brings it under that law is a question for a lawyer.

The deaths on record in this trade happen in tanks and in closed rooms. Two housekeeping staff died in Delhi in 2019 cleaning a restaurant's kitchen-waste treatment unit, reportedly without safety equipment ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/delhi/2-die-while-cleaning-restaurant-s-kitchen-waste-treatment-unit-747631)), and the Supreme Court raised compensation for sewer deaths to Rs 30 lakh in 2023 ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/supreme-court/completely-eradicate-manual-scavenging-supreme-court-directs-union-states-increases-compensation-for-sewer-deaths-to-rs-30-lakh-240650)). In Chennai in November 2024 two children died after a pest-control firm applied rat poison in a closed, air-conditioned home, and two employees were arrested ([ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/en/!state/chennai-kids-die-inhaling-toxic-fumes-rat-poison-pesticide-firm-kundrathur-enn24111501827)). Three written rules remove the worst cases: no ECCS worker enters a tank, pit or chamber; no fumigants; no treatment of a closed, occupied room. The grease-trap service also makes ECCS the holder of the waste, and the Hyderabad water board fined a hotel Rs 10,000 for dumping waste into a manhole ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/hyderabad-hotel-fined-rs-10k-for-dumping-waste-after-malakpet-sewer-inspection-3228674/)), so a lawful disposal route comes before the first sale. For everything else the usual protection is three insurance policies (public liability, employees' compensation, and professional indemnity for the inspection score). No premium for this trade was found in any published source; three broker quotes are the only way to price it.

The hygiene score and public QR page carry a different kind of risk. FSSAI has said that nobody may use its name or logo and that only government officials may inspect premises to check licences ([Food Safety Works](https://foodsafetyworks.com/?p=10614)); the food safety law sets a penalty of up to Rs 10 lakh for an advertisement likely to mislead about quality ([Food Safety and Standards Act, section 53](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/foodsafety/53.php)); and the consumer authority's 2022 rules say an endorsement must reflect a "reasonably current" opinion and that any connection between endorser and business "should be fully disclosed" ([Vikaspedia](https://en.vikaspedia.in/social-welfare/social-awareness/consumer-education/guidelines-for-prevention-of-misleading-advertisements-and-endorsements-for-misleading-advertisements-2022)). No Indian authority's statement about private hygiene scores was found, and that silence is not clearance. The practical consequences are to avoid the words "certified" and "hygiene rating" and anything resembling FSSAI's imagery, to show the inspection date and let the page expire, and to say on the page that the restaurant pays ECCS. There is also a conflict of interest that no wording removes. India's own food-audit regulations bar an agency from auditing a business it has trained or advised in the past two years ([Food Safety Auditing Regulations 2018](https://www.casemine.com/act/in/5f5f126f9fca195c05ccf2db)), so a score issued by the firm paid to clean the kitchen is a supplier's own assessment. It works as proof of work and as a management tool; it is weak as a public trust mark, which limits any growth story built on the badge.

Two smaller items complete the picture. India's new data-protection duties start around 13 May 2027 ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/lawschool/articles/compliance-without-court-what-dpdpa-board-vacancy-means-may2027-549463)), but a 2022 direction already requires any company to report a cyber incident within six hours and keep system logs for 180 days in India ([Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/2022-cert-in-directions-on-reporting-cyber-incidents/)); a server on one office PC cannot meet that and must move to proper hosting before any restaurant's staff are enrolled. And collecting small invoices through the law is slow: the government's delayed-payment portal shows 256,892 applications since 2017 and 63,099 disposed ([MSME Samadhaan](https://samadhaan.msme.gov.in/MyMsme/MSEFC/MSEFC_Welcome.aspx)), so subscriptions should be paid in advance and one-off jobs on the day. The researcher's overall judgement is that nothing here is a reason not to proceed. Tank entry, fumigant pest control and an official-looking badge are to be designed out; registration, lawful wages, insurance and hosting are ordinary costs of doing business. Almost none of those costs could be priced from published sources.

## Y Combinator offers US$500,000 and asks for a foreign parent and three months abroad

### The terms and the deadline

YC runs four three-month batches a year, in person in San Francisco. **The next on-time deadline is 2 November 2026 at 8pm Pacific time for the Winter 2027 batch, which runs from January to March 2027**, with decisions by 11 December ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/apply)). By the researcher's calculation that is 9:30am on 3 November in India. Late applications are still read, with no promised reply date, and no deadline for later batches has been published. A company need not be registered to apply ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)). The investment is US$500,000 in two parts ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/deal)); the researchers used exchange rates from Rs 85 to Rs 94.5 to the dollar, none taken from a bank, which puts it at very roughly Rs 4.25 crore to Rs 4.7 crore. The first US$125,000 buys a fixed 7% of the company. The other US$375,000 converts into shares later at the best price any other investor gets, with no ceiling on the company's value agreed in advance. YC's own example gives it a further 2.5% if the next investors value the company at US$15 million. The researcher's arithmetic for lower valuations, which are likelier for a local Indian services business, gives a further 7.5% at US$5 million and 12.5% at US$3 million, so **YC's total stake would sit in the mid-teens of percent** before the next round takes its own share. If the founder never raised money again, both parts would stay outstanding with no end date, and YC's pages do not say what happens then; that question belongs with a lawyer before signing anything.

### What YC selects on, and its record with founders like this one

YC's written position is kind to two parts of this founder's profile and hard on a third. It says "we regularly accept solo founders", while advising that "one-person startups are tough", and that "on average, 40% of the companies we fund in each batch are just an idea" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)). The same page says the founding team must be able to build the product itself, and "for most businesses, that usually means you need a technical co-founder". The one partner statement found on AI-written code cuts against a non-engineer: Jared Friedman said of the Winter 2025 companies whose code was almost all written by AI, "It's not like we funded a bunch of non-technical founders. Every one of these people is highly technical" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/06/a-quarter-of-startups-in-ycs-current-cohort-have-codebases-that-are-almost-entirely-ai-generated/)). That quote is from March 2025, and no later statement on solo founders, non-engineers or India was found before the searches ran out.

The numbers are discouraging. YC states that more than 10,000 companies apply every three months and about 1% are accepted ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/investors)). An outside count found 22 solo founders among 199 companies in Winter 2026, 11% ([Extruct AI](https://www.extruct.ai/research/ycw26)). Press counts of Indian companies fell from several dozen a year in 2020-2022 to four in 2024 and one in 2025; that one, Bolna, said YC rejected its first five applications because "it didn't see the potential to make money from enterprise customers in India" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/startup-accelerator-y-combinator-indian-startup); [Asia Tech Review](https://www.asiatechreview.com/p/y-combinator-reaps-big-rewards-in)). YC's own directory shows somewhat more India-based companies in 2025 and 2026, almost all of them AI or developer tools ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/location/india)). No YC company was found, in any country, that sells commercial kitchen cleaning, duct cleaning, pest control or food-safety inspection as a service. The two nearest precedents were not beginners: Hogaru was already cleaning offices in three Colombian cities when it entered, and Craftwork, a house-painting company, was started by four people including a founder on his second YC company and an experienced engineer ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/yc-w17-launch-hogaru-elemeno-health-playment-hivy-and-bulk-mro/); [Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/craftwork)). The comparison a partner would hold in mind for ECCS's software half is Marble, an AI-first product for restaurant back-of-house in the Summer 2026 batch ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/restaurant-tech)). ECCS has no AI feature built, and the project's own study of where AI fits found uses that save office and supervisor time (reading documents, translating, drafting inspection reports) and none that replaces the cleaning itself.

The researcher's reading of the odds is a judgement and not a statistic. It starts from 1% and moves down for each of: one founder, not an engineer in YC's sense, an India-only market, a labour-heavy service, no user, no AI in the product and no company. Nothing found moves it up except the speed and completeness of the build. The fair statement is "well under one in a hundred, and lower still for Winter 2027"; no source supports a more exact number. Four obstacles cannot be fixed in the three weeks to the deadline: the India-only market, the labour-heavy model, the foreign-parent rule described next, and the three months away from the business.

### What a foreign parent company would involve

YC invests only in companies registered in the United States, Canada, Singapore or the Cayman Islands, and a company registered elsewhere must "create a parent company that is in one of those jurisdictions" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)). For ECCS that means a foreign parent owning an Indian company that employs the crews. Forming the parent is cheap and quick: one service charges US$500 and then US$100 a year ([Stripe Atlas](https://stripe.com/atlas)). The costs lie elsewhere. Indian rules treat even a zero-capital foreign company as an overseas investment to be reported through a bank before it is made, followed by yearly returns with late fees ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/fema-odi-rules-and-regulations/)). More seriously, one law firm's reading of the 2022 rules is that an Indian resident may control a foreign company only if it "does not have a subsidiary" ([Obhan & Associates in Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/view-point/significant-reforms-in-the-indian-overseas-investment-regime)), while another adviser describes a Delaware parent over an Indian company as the standard, permitted structure ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/legal/fema-odi-rules-and-regulations/)). **The two sources conflict, and this must go to a lawyer who practises Indian exchange-control law before anything is formed.** There is also the cost of coming home: a Delaware company cannot list on an Indian exchange, Groww paid US$159.4 million in US tax to move its parent back to India, and the same adviser calls a foreign parent for a business whose revenue is entirely Indian "deferred cost accumulation with no corresponding benefit" ([Treelife](https://treelife.in/case-studies/how-growws-160-million-delaware-tax-bill-became-indias-most-expensive-startup-lesson/)). Groww's bill belongs to a very large company; the direction of advice is what matters here.

The practical side is no easier. A founder accepted in mid-December needs a US visa by January, and refusals are documented: YC itself said in 2023 that one company's founders had their visas "denied twice" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/19/yc-says-visa-challenges-hampering-participation-of-international-founders)), and a Bengaluru founder reported in February 2026 that his visitor visa was refused after questions about his minimal salary ([Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/trending/bengaluru-startup-founder-flags-us-visa-rejection-sparks-debate-on-financial-scrutiny-of-entrepreneurs-88842.htm)). Three months in San Francisco are also three months in which the only founder of a business delivered by crews in Hyderabad kitchens is not there. Against all this, applying costs only time, and YC says "about half the companies applied multiple times before being accepted" ([Y Combinator](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)). No data shows how much an early, weak application helps a later one.

## India's realistic first money is a subsidised loan, a grant and customers' own payments

The well-known Indian programmes are open and writing large cheques, and nearly all are aimed elsewhere. Antler India offers Rs 4 crore for 11% but asks for full-time presence in Bengaluru and puts AI-first companies first ([Antler India](https://antler.co/location/india)). Peak XV's Surge invests US$500,000 to US$5 million, and in its September 2026 intake only 5 of 18 companies focused on the Indian market ([Surge](https://surge.peakxv.com/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3170634)). Accel's 2026 Atoms cohort chose five AI companies "from thousands of applicants" ([Accel](https://accel.com/noteworthies/google-joins-accel-to-turbocharge-ai-founders-with-atoms-ai-cohort-2026)). None states an interest in a local, crew-based services business. The names with a better fit are a few funds and networks that write a company's first outside cheque. India Quotient says "the best time to pitch is when you haven't got a single customer, and the best form of pitching is a link to your product" ([India Quotient](https://www.indiaquotient.in/)). Titan Capital lists home services in its portfolio ([Titan Capital](https://www.titancapital.vc/)). Inflection Point Ventures led a seed round of about US$2 million in Clean Fanatics, a home deep-cleaning company, in July 2025 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/snippets/clean-fanatics-raises-2-mn-in-seed-round-led-by-inflection-point-ventures-9480496)). Hyderabad Angels invests Rs 50 lakh to Rs 4 crore, at a stage it calls "Seed+" ([Hyderabad Angels](https://hyderabadangels.in/)). Every one of them invests in registered companies, so none can fund ECCS today, and Hyderabad's state-backed institutions are mostly for deep technology: T-Hub's named programmes cover semiconductors, AI, space, mobility and healthcare ([T-Hub](https://t-hub.co/)).

Government money is cheaper and closer to what this business needs, though every scheme also requires a registered entity first. Startup recognition from the central government needs a private limited company, a limited liability partnership or a registered partnership ([Startup India](https://www.startupindia.gov.in/content/sih/en/startupgov/startup_recognition_page.html)). With it, a company less than two years old can apply through incubators for the Startup India Seed Fund: a grant of up to Rs 20 lakh for trials and up to Rs 50 lakh as debt, for "technology-based" startups, according to lender and adviser guides ([Bajaj Finserv](https://www.bajajfinserv.in/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/startup-india-seed-fund-scheme-sisfs-grant-apply)). Whether a committee sees a technology company or a cleaning contractor decides ECCS's chance, and whether the scheme has been formally extended was not confirmed. For a plain services start two central schemes fit better. Under PMEGP, lender guides describe a bank loan for a new service unit of up to Rs 20 lakh with a 15% to 25% subsidy in urban areas ([L&T Finance](https://www.ltfinance.com/blog/business-loan/pmegp-loan)), which the researcher works out as Rs 3 lakh to Rs 5 lakh. MUDRA offers small-business loans in tiers up to Rs 20 lakh, according to a lender's blog ([OkCredit](https://okcredit.in/blog/government-policies-for-small-businesses/)). Neither was confirmed on an official page. The business is cheap to start by startup standards; one guide puts a commercial cleaning operation with specialised equipment at "Rs 5 lakh or more" ([IIFL](https://www.iifl.com/blogs/gold-loan/how-to-start-a-cleaning-service-in-delhi-cost-licence-setup-guide)), a figure written for Delhi that leaves out wages and a vehicle. Ordinary lenders come later: the earliest one found asks for a year of trading ([Indifi](https://www.indifi.com/)), and revenue-based lenders start at Rs 1 crore of yearly revenue ([Recur Club](https://www.recurclub.com/)).

Partners may matter more than investors. Swiggy's hygiene programme says it offers restaurants "exclusive pricing on third-party cleaning and pest control services" ([Swiggy](https://www.swiggy.com/corporate/?p=6650)), and the Hyderabad chapter of the restaurant association formed a food-safety task force in May 2024 that promised hygiene audits "with third-party auditors" ([Siasat](https://www.siasat.com/nrai-hyderabad-forms-task-force-to-ensure-food-safety-in-restaurants-after-raids-3035224/amp/)). Whether either arrangement is active in Hyderabad in 2026 was not confirmed. ## The verdict: a sound local business if the pilot pays, and a YC application only in 2027

### Viability in India and abroad

In India, ECCS is viable as a specialised local services company and unproven as anything larger. The case for the local business is that enforcement has made closure a real cost, inspectors ask for exactly the records the service produces, restaurants already pay outsiders for each piece, no rival sells them together, the specialised end of the trade earns good margins elsewhere, and a buyer for such firms exists. The case against is that not one of the numbers that decide it has been measured for Hyderabad: the price, the share of restaurants that buy on a schedule, the renewal rate, and how many visits a crew can make in a day. As a venture-scale company it is not viable on present evidence, because one city cannot supply the revenue, the labour does not automate, the app is not a barrier, and no investor has funded the category. As a software product sold by itself to Indian restaurants it is not viable; the market leader earns under Rs 8,000 a client a year. Abroad, ECCS is not viable as an exported service at all. The Gulf is a credible later market through a licensed local partner, and software-only sales elsewhere would enter a crowded field whose best documented exit took a decade.

### The conditions under which it works

The local business works if the paid pilot shows that most restaurants pay full price and renew at the first renewal without a discount; that each visit leaves a gross margin nearer the specialist's 40% than the manpower firm's 15%; that customers sit close enough together for a crew to make several stops a day; that most revenue comes from scheduled plans paid in advance; that the company is registered, insured and paying lawful wages; and that someone with real experience of the trade is supervising quality. These thresholds are the researcher's reading of what buyers of such businesses pay for abroad, untested in Hyderabad. The venture case needs all of that and three things more: a second city that works without the founder present, a route to tens of thousands of outlets or to much larger customers, and something that builds up over time which a copier cannot get quickly, such as a year of inspection records across hundreds of kitchens. If restaurants will not pay and renew, neither path is open and no further software will change that.

### Whether and when to apply to Y Combinator

The direct answer is no for Winter 2027 as a serious bid, and never if the aim is a profitable local company. For a founder who wants venture scale, the serious application belongs to a 2027 batch whose deadline falls after the pilot's first renewals. On YC's usual rhythm that points to Summer or Fall 2027, and YC has published no dates for either. The batch named is this report's own inference from that rhythm. That application should carry paying restaurants with renewal and margin figures, a co-founder who is an engineer or a seasoned operator, a market beyond Hyderabad, an AI function that does real work, a lawyer's clearance on the foreign-parent structure, and a valid US visa. A short, honest application by 2 November does no harm if it takes two or three days and does not delay the first paying customers; its value is the discipline of writing the pitch, and it changes nothing about the odds. If the founder already runs a trading cleaning business, that changes: apply on time, with the numbers. In no case should a company be formed abroad in order to apply, since YC does not require one at application.

### The recommended path

The path is the same for the first two months whichever aim the founder holds, and it is ordered so that the cheapest steps answer the biggest questions first.

| When | Step | What it settles |
|---|---|---|
| This week | Write down the three answers: is there a trading business, what is the founder's history in the trade, and is the aim venture money or a profitable local company | Which half of this report applies |
| This week and next | Phone five vendors for real quotes (Rentokil PCI, HiCare, Dasha and two local operators) and interview ten to twenty restaurant operators on what they pay now, to whom and how often | Replaces the two unmeasured inputs behind the market size and the margins |
| Weeks 1 to 4 | Offer a free scored inspection as the door-opener, and get three to five restaurants in one or two neighbourhoods to pay for a first clean, pest visit or plan | Turns "no one has used it" into "launched", and produces the first real prices |
| When the first paid job is agreed | Register a private limited company, which keeps investors, the Seed Fund grant and startup recognition open; add Shops and Establishments registration and Udyam (small-business) registration; apply for the PMEGP subsidy during set-up if it is wanted | Removes the block on contracts, GST invoices, insurance and store accounts |

## Conclusion

The research turns two separate questions into one experiment. Whether ECCS is a business, and whether it is the kind of business an investor or an accelerator would back, are both answered by the same four numbers from the same paid pilot, and none of them can be found on the web. That is why the sharpest finding here is about time and not money. The three weeks before YC's deadline are worth more spent getting five restaurants to pay than spent describing a company that has no customers, and the decision about YC is really a decision about what kind of company the founder wants, which only the founder can make.

The week-long build deserves a second look in that light. It shows that software for this trade has become cheap enough for one person to make, which removes the usual reason a services founder needs outside capital, and for the same reason it gives no protection. What cannot be built in a week is what the comparables say carries the value: crews that stay, customers packed into a few streets, and a dated record of every kitchen that an inspector, a chain's head office or a buyer of the company will one day ask to see. If the pilot produces those, ECCS will have choices it does not have today, including a much stronger application to anyone. If it does not, the founder will have learned that for a few lakh rupees and a few months, before giving up any share of the company.
